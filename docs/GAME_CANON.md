# REN — The Twelfth Hour / Game Canon

## Core premise

REN is a third-person mythological action-RPG rooted in ancient Egyptian religion, funerary belief, writing, names, memory, and the Duat.

The protagonist, **Nefer**, is a deceased Egyptian scribe.

He is not secretly Ra, a reincarnated god, or a chosen superhero. His power and tragedy are tied to writing, erasure, memory, and responsibility.

## Nefer

Nefer was involved in erasing names in life.

His fragmented memory after death is not cheap amnesia. He deliberately participated in arranging his own fragmentation as part of a seal meant to contain something dangerous.

His journey reconstructs both his identity and the dangerous formula hidden inside that identity.

End-state:
- Nefer does not become Ra.
- Nefer becomes an **Akh**, framed as Keeper of Names / He Who Remembers.

## Soul progression

Progression is structured around Egyptian concepts:

1. Khat — body
2. Sheut — shadow
3. Ka — vital force
4. Ib — heart
5. Ba — mobile personality
6. Ren — true name
7. Akh — completed/transfigured state

These are narrative and mechanical milestones, not generic stat loot.

## Meru

Meru is Nefer's older mentor / lector-priest figure.

In life he was trusted.

His manipulation is central:
- Nefer's fragments contain pieces of a dangerous semantic formula.
- Meru engineers circumstances so Nefer unknowingly reconstructs it.
- Core reveal line concept: Meru did not collect Nefer's pieces; Nefer collected them for him.

Meru should not read as a cartoon villain. His calm authority and familiarity matter.

## Apep

Apep is recurring chaos.

Apep must never be permanently killed by the player.

Combat with Apep is resistance, survival, interruption, containment, or participation in the nightly struggle — never a permanent "boss kill."

## The Unname

Final antagonistic concept:
**The Unname** — semantic erasure assembled from stolen identities / names.

It is not just a monster. It is the threat of identity becoming unrecorded, unremembered, and semantically absent.

## Boss-reward rule

- Monsters killed → Ren Glyph / Invocation
- Gods defeated but not killed → Divine Mark
- Soul guardians → restore part of Nefer
- Narrative bosses → mechanic + revelation

Avoid generic loot such as "+8% poison resistance" as the core reward language.

## Twelve chapters / Twelve Hours

The game is structured around the twelve hours of the Amduat.

1. Gate That Forgot Its Name — Face-Eater
2. Shadow That Fled — Kheft / Sheut
3. Silent House of Life — Hori / memory reed
4. Red Land Remembers — Red Storm Beast + Seth trial
5. Beneath Rosetau — serpent + Sokar / Ka
6. Heart on the Scale — Ammit + Osiris / Ib
7. Black Register — Forgotten King + Choir / REMEMBER
8. Broken Eye — Horus / Ba + Wedjat
9. Burning Eye — Sekhmet
10. Boat That Stopped — Drowned Host + Knot of Apep / Ren
11. Man Outside the Book — Meru
12. Twelfth Hour — Mouth of Apep + The Unname / Akh

## Character guardrails

### Nefer
- deceased Egyptian scribe
- lean, human scale
- funerary linen rather than fantasy armor
- no projected human shadow until Sheut is recovered
- Reed Blade is his iconic weapon

### Reed Blade
- scribal-reed-derived ritual blade
- dark bronze spine
- obsidian cutting inserts
- acacia grip
- linen wrap
- restrained faience
- not a bow, club, khopesh, wicker weapon, or generic fantasy sword

### Anubis
- tall and lean
- integrated black jackal/golden-wolf head
- human hands
- clean off-white linen
- minimal electrum
- stillness is power
- not a generic muscular jackal warrior

### Seth
- distinct Set-animal, not jackal/wolf/dog
- athletic human body
- reddish-brown/charcoal short fur on head
- elongated narrow downward-curving snout
- tall squared ears
- red ochre sash
- restrained bronze
- anti-Apep spear

### Ra at night
- nocturnal ram-headed king
- ancient, exhausted divine dignity
- dim amber solar disk
- aged linen / sacred gold accents
- not a bald human priest

### Horus
- falcon-headed warrior king
- no permanent giant angel wings

### Isis
- human Egyptian woman
- elegant Heka/scribal magic
- not generic fantasy queen styling

### Osiris
- regal stillness
- subdued green divine skin
- funerary linen

### Thoth
- ibis-headed slender scribe

### Sekhmet
- integrated lioness head
- heat distortion, not generic fire aura

## Dialogue tone

Egyptian Arabic can be used for VO, but it should be restrained, serious, and timeless rather than comedic or modern-street slang.

Examples of current tonal direction:
- Meru: "طول ما اسمك مكتوب… مش هتموت."
- Anubis: "اسمك."
- Nefer: "مش فاكر."
- Anubis: "يبقى حد قتلك مرتين."

Treat wording as editable script, not immutable canon, unless explicitly locked later.
