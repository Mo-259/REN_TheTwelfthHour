# REN — Cinematic & Trailer Direction

## Goal

Cinematics should feel captured inside the same playable Unreal world.

The player should never feel that gameplay cuts to a different AI/movie universe.

## Direction language

Reference philosophy:
- psychological precision and unsettling imagery
- strong AAA gameplay payoff
- short, authored shots
- controlled framing
- physical continuity

Do not copy another game's shot design directly.

## Shot rule

**One shot = one visual idea + one primary physical action.**

Avoid:
- complex 10-second choreography
- floating cameras
- dream interpolation
- excessive camera movement
- multiple unrelated actions in one shot

## Camera

Gameplay:
- stable third-person follow
- predictable framing
- minimal DOF

Hero shot:
- intentional fixed camera or controlled move
- no arbitrary orbit
- no geometry changes for framing

## Sequencer

Use Sequencer for:
- Tomb exit reveal
- Anubis encounter
- boss intro if needed
- controlled final beat

Do not use Sequencer to fake traversal that should be gameplay.

## Gameplay-to-cinematic transitions

Preferred:
- transition camera organically from gameplay position
- preserve player world position
- preserve prop and light continuity
- give control back quickly

## Trailer principle

The final trailer should prove that a game exists.

Therefore include enough unmistakable real gameplay:
- traversal
- interaction
- combat
- boss response

Hero cinematics support gameplay; they do not replace it.

## Audio direction

Important textures:
- reed on papyrus
- breath
- stone
- linen
- heartbeat
- low vocal funerary textures
- frame drums used with restraint
- bowed metal
- low strings
- whispers

Avoid relying on cliché instrumentation as the entire Egyptian identity.
