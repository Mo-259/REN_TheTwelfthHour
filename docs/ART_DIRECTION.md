# REN — Art Direction Bible

## North star

Ancient Egypt first.
Mythology second.
Fantasy third.

The game should feel as if a real ancient Egyptian funerary worldview became physically playable.

Avoid "Egypt-themed fantasy."

## Rendering

Target:
- premium AAA real-time rendering
- physically based materials
- gameplay readability
- stable exposure
- readable midtones
- controlled darkness

Gameplay is not graded like an almost-black film.

## Material library

Primary:
- limestone
- sandstone
- basalt
- granite
- cedar
- acacia
- linen
- aged leather
- bronze
- copper
- faience
- papyrus
- painted plaster

Gold:
- sacred accent only
- never blanket decoration

## Palette

- limestone ivory
- desaturated sand
- charcoal
- basalt gray
- muted Egyptian blue
- malachite
- red ochre
- Nile blue-black
- amber

## Heka visual language

Allowed:
- living hieroglyphs
- moving black ink
- carved symbols carrying restrained mineral light
- names physically disappearing
- shadows behaving incorrectly
- spoken names affecting matter
- funerary material seams

Reject:
- neon beams
- digital glitches
- generic energy circles
- floating fantasy runes
- superhero particle storms

## Duat

Impossible physics may exist, but materials remain physical.

Examples:
- black river overhead
- stars below the ground
- vertical tomb city
- unsupported doors
- suspended sarcophagi

The impossible should look architecturally and materially real.

## Gameplay mode

- camera ~3–5m behind player
- 35–40mm equivalent feel
- minimal depth of field
- open midtones
- clear route
- readable character silhouette
- readable materials

## Hero moments

Can become:
- more monumental
- slower
- stronger composition
- deeper negative space

But must remain the same game world.

## Character guardrail

No oversized fantasy armor.
No generic bodybuilding.
No sci-fi ornament.
No random glowing tattoos unless narratively justified.

Nefer remains visually a scribe/dead human, not a fantasy king.
